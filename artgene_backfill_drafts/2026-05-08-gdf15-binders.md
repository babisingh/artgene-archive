# AI-designed binders to GDF15 for a cachexia biosensor and a decoy therapeutic

*A KAIST group combines scaffold grafting, RFdiffusion and a scaffold-search method to reach picomolar GDF15 binders, then builds a one-step luminescent sensor and an Fc decoy as potent as a clinical antibody.*

**What was done.** Jinsook Ahn, Ryeongeun Cho and colleagues led by Ho Min Kim at the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology published this work in Experimental and Molecular Medicine, online 8 May 2026. It is peer reviewed. GDF15 is a cytokine that rises in cancer cachexia and drives weight loss, and the authors wanted synthetic binders to both detect and block it. They compared three ways to make binders: grafting a motif from the natural receptor GFRAL, de novo backbone generation with RFdiffusion, and a scaffold-search and grafting approach that mines the Protein Data Bank for structural analogs to reach the shallow, polar site B. ProteinMPNN wrote the sequences and AlphaFold2 and AlphaFold3 filtered the designs.

**What they made and measured.** Twenty-four designs were selected for the bench. Scaffold grafting plus one round of partial diffusion reached SG2-4 at 300 picomolar, a 300-fold gain over the natural receptor motif. De novo diffusion produced three-helix bundles with low nanomolar affinity, led by DE3 at 7.6 nanomolar. The scaffold-search route produced SSG2 at 121 nanomolar against the difficult site B. At least 13 designs bound GDF15 measurably by surface plasmon resonance. The de novo binder DE3 was fused to split NanoLuc to make a one-step, wash-free luminescent sensor that reads human and mouse GDF15 in serum, and SG2-4 was built into an Fc decoy with an IC50 of 7.2 nanomolar in a cell signaling assay, comparable to the clinical antibody ponsegromab.

**What it shows.** Pairing a scaffold-search strategy with diffusion and grafting let the team reach a concave, polar epitope that de novo diffusion alone could not, and turn the binders into both a diagnostic and a therapeutic modality from fully computational designs.

**Caveats.** No atomic structure of any designed complex was solved, so binding modes remain modeled. Several designs failed to express or aggregated, affinity maturation of the de novo series did not improve on the parent, and the therapeutic data are in vitro only.

**Why it matters for provenance.** Several binders are fully de novo with low identity to any natural protein. The designed sequences are given in the supplementary information and were not deposited to a database, so an external registry would have to capture them from the paper.
